B2B · Subscription · US Market Confidential

U.S. Virtual Office Provider

United States · B2B Services · Client details confidential

Inherited an underperforming Meta & Google Ads account with a volatile CPA of $580 — unsustainable for a subscription business model where customer lifetime value, not immediate revenue, is the correct return metric. Over 6 months, I diagnosed why an existing video-first creative strategy wasn't converting: analysed organic and paid video performance to identify what content attributes were actually driving purchase intent, and fed those findings directly to the head of marketing to inform future production decisions. This intelligence layer unlocked a structured A/B testing framework across hooks, angles, and offers — and informed city-level segmentation for high-value US markets. Campaign optimisation was governed by a start, stop and scale framework I developed, which replaced arbitrary time-based cycles with performance-triggered decisions. CPA stabilised in the $100–150 target range. The account reached 312 purchases in its peak month.

Note on ROAS methodology: Immediate revenue ROAS was 1.56x — typical for subscription models where most value is recovered over the customer lifetime. LTV-adjusted ROAS of 9.35x reflects the estimated lifetime value of 1,464 customers acquired over the engagement period.

About
Paul Omolo · Founder, Craftivo

Most businesses don't have a paid media problem.

They have a discipline problem — campaigns built on gut feel, optimised by hope, and measured too late. I started Craftivo because I kept seeing the same thing: good businesses bleeding money on campaigns nobody was really watching.

I watch the numbers every day. That's the whole job.

Over the past decade I've managed campaigns across three continents — cut a client's cost per acquisition by 82%, and delivered a 16x return on a budget most agencies wouldn't prioritise, while managing over $188K in ad spend for a US client who needed someone who'd actually show up for the numbers. I don't think most businesses need more ad spend. They need someone who loses sleep when the CPA creeps up on a Tuesday.

The work is in the details. Always has been.
